# Data classification

`CLAUDE.md` requires that customer energy data and uploaded evidence be treated as
sensitive, and the charter promises security and tenant isolation by default. This
document defines the categories, their handling, and the rules that apply before
any real data exists — which is the only time such rules are cheap to adopt.

## Why energy data is sensitive

Interval energy data is not a neutral meter total. At Site 001's sampling
cadence, a load series reveals:

- when the dwelling is occupied and when it is empty
- sleep and waking schedules
- when someone cooks, showers, or does laundry
- the presence of medical or assistive equipment
- when the dwelling was left for an extended period

That is a behavioural record of a household, and Site 001 is a real two-bedroom
apartment where people live. The platform's own customers include property
managers and landlords, so tenant load data is data about one party held by
another — which is exactly why isolation is a default and not a feature.

## Categories

| Class | Definition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C0 — Public** | Intended for anyone | This documentation, the charter |
| **C1 — Internal** | Non-sensitive operational detail | Schema definitions, synthetic fixtures, aggregate platform metrics |
| **C2 — Customer sensitive** | Reveals a customer's site, behaviour, or equipment | Interval readings, events, site addresses, panel photos, nameplate images, equipment inventories, tenant identities |
| **C3 — Secret** | Grants access or impersonation | Credentials, API keys, tokens, gateway keys, certificates, private keys |

Where classification is unclear, treat data as the higher class. Interval
telemetry from a real site is **always C2**, however coarse.

## Handling

| | C0 | C1 | C2 | C3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In the repository | Yes | Yes | **Never** | **Never** |
| In fixtures | Yes | Yes | **Never** | **Never** |
| In logs | Yes | Yes | Identifiers only, never values | **Never** |
| In error messages | Yes | Yes | Identifiers only | **Never** |
| In an issue, PR, or screenshot | Yes | Yes | **Never** | **Never** |
| Sent to any third party | Yes | Case by case | Requires approval | **Never** |

### Rules

**D-1 — No real site data in the repository, ever.** No capture from Site 001 or
any real site is committed, in any form, including "anonymized". A single named
site cannot be anonymized: the site is identified by being the only one. This is
the rule most likely to be broken with good intentions — debugging a real outage
with a real capture — and the reason the simulator is scenario-driven, so a real
situation can be *reproduced* rather than *pasted in*.

**D-2 — Fixtures are synthetic.** Generated by the simulator from a seed, or
hand-written. No real address, occupant name, account number, meter serial, or
coordinate appears in any fixture. Tested.

**D-3 — Logs carry identifiers, not values.** `site_001` and `readingId` are
loggable; `load.site.active_power = 1840 W` at a timestamp is a behavioural
observation and is not. Structured logging makes this a field-selection
discipline rather than a formatting accident.

**D-4 — Secrets never enter the repository.** Local ignored environment files
only, per `secrets-and-configuration.md`. `.gitignore` already covers `.env`,
`.env.*`, `*.pem`, `*.key`, `*.p12`, `*.pfx`.

**D-5 — Evidence uploads are C2 on arrival.** Panel photos and nameplate images
carry more than their subject: location metadata, reflections, adjacent
documents, and occupants. When object storage is introduced, EXIF stripping and
access control are part of that work, not a follow-up.

**D-6 — Provenance is not a privacy control.** A `simulated` origin makes data safe to
commit; an `imported` or `measured` origin does not by itself make data sensitive, and
neither does any `derivation` value. The two systems are orthogonal and must not be
conflated — `../architecture/data-provenance.md` governs truthfulness, this document
governs exposure.

## Retention

> **TO CONFIRM (D15)** — retention periods. Proposed starting positions:
>
> | Data | Proposed retention |
> | --- | --- |
> | Raw interval readings | 24 months, then downsample to hourly aggregates |
> | Derived events | Indefinite — the operational record is the product |
> | Evidence uploads | Life of the customer relationship, then delete on request |
> | Logs containing identifiers | 90 days |
> | Synthetic fixtures | Indefinite; not customer data |
>
> Slice 0 stores only simulated data locally, so nothing binds yet. This needs
> deciding before the first real reading is stored, not after — retention adopted
> retroactively means deleting data customers have already come to rely on.

Tenants and owners are different parties with different claims to the same
readings. Any retention or deletion policy has to answer whose request governs;
that question is open and belongs with D15.

## Tenant isolation

Deferred with authentication (D16), specified now so it is not retrofitted:

- Every query is scoped by organization at the data-access layer, not by
  filtering in the application. Filtering that a caller can forget is not
  isolation.
- No identifier is guessable across tenants in a way that leaks existence.
- A property manager's access to a tenant's data is an explicit, recorded,
  revocable grant — not an implication of owning the building. The charter names
  both as customers; the platform must not silently take the landlord's side.

## Incident handling

No process exists yet, and none is needed while all data is simulated. Before the
first real reading is stored, this document needs: who is notified, what the CNMI
and any applicable US notification obligations are, and how affected parties are
contacted. Flagged as an open item rather than left implicit.

## Slice 0 position

Everything Slice 0 touches is **C1**. All readings carry `origins: ["simulated"]`, all
fixtures are synthetic, there are no credentials, and no real site data exists anywhere
in the system. C2 handling becomes live the moment a verified device produces the first
reading with `measured` among its origins — which is the moment this document stops
being preparatory.
